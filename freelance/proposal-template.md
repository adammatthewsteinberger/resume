# Proposal template

Keep a proposal to about 150–250 words. The client is the subject of the first sentence, not me. Every proposal is written and sent by a person; no tool bids on my behalf.

**Before writing, check the brief.** Take the job only if the brief states an outcome, a rough size and a decision-maker. A vague brief that pays well is still a risk to the Job Success Score; reply with the clarifying question below instead of a full proposal, or pass.

```text
Hi {first name},

You need {their outcome, in their words}, {by their date if they gave one}.

I've done this before: {one proof line from SERVICES.md, trimmed to one sentence}.

How I'd approach it:
1. {first step, usually a short written review of what exists}
2. {the build or assessment itself}
3. {handoff: documentation and a walkthrough for whoever owns it}

You'd get {the deliverables, as a short list}, at a fixed price of {price}. It's done when {the two or three items that would go on the acceptance checklist}.

One question before I scope it exactly: {the single unknown that most changes the price or the plan}.

I work written-first, so you can answer on your own time; a call is fine if you'd prefer one.

Adam
```

## When the brief is too vague to price

```text
Hi {first name},

Thanks for posting this. Before I send a price, I'd like to be sure I'm solving the right problem: {one specific question about the outcome or the scope}.

With that answered, I can send a fixed scope and price the same day I reply.

Adam
```
